Round-5: The Defect Was the Emission Policy, Not the Features

Reproduction first. scripts/cache_fold_models.py re-trained raw19 / multi25 / multi30 / random02 on the frozen masked protocol and matched evidence/holdout_masked.json on every fold of every arm to full float64 precision (multi25 = 0.09071277311870418). Rounds 1–4 are verified reproducible. That verification exposed the real defect: on the same protocol the uniform random 2 % control scores 0.13788 — better than every learned arm we have built.

The official metric gives a truth pixel credit if any predicted pixel falls within 300 m (3 px) of it, and takes max over that 5 × 5 cell. Two predictions inside one cell therefore add false-positive mass and add zero true-positive credit. Every round 1–4 arm — and the archived 06ca61e5 artifact — emitted top-k by probability, which piles mass exactly where it is wasted. The sparsest emission that can still credit every truth pixel is one with no two predictions in a 5 × 5 block: non-maximum suppression at radius 2, density 1/25.

Mechanism, quantified: at the same emitted mass (≈23 k px per fold) top-k credits 7.6–15.0 % of truth pixels while NMS-3 credits 24.6–29.1 % — a per-fold coverage gain of 1.9–3.2× for the same false-positive bill. Evidence: emission probe (JSON) · off-catalogue check (JSON) · Round-5 gate (JSON).

Round-7: Sub-100 m Topography — the First Material Feature Gain

H32 sub-100 m scarp signature cleared its preregistered gate on every fold of both protocols (register, written before code). Twelve label-free channels per 100 m cell from the USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second (~10 m) DEM — built on a GitHub runner by sibling GEMSDOE10 (tag ext/dem10-36343078537), re-hashed here tile-by-tile and channel-by-channel (dem10-fetch.json). Frozen masked holdout, NMS-3 @2 %, leakage probe 0.0000 every fold: dense 0.24586 vs incumbent 0.22143 (+0.0244, 4/4); sparse 0.12732 vs 0.11438 (+0.0129, 4/4). The reference arms reproduced Round-6 to the last digit (multi25 0.22138 / 0.11178). Ablation without H28: 0.24658 / 0.12706 — H28 is irrelevant either way; the preregistered primary is shipped. Artifact 12GEMSDOE_r7-nms3-dem10-scarp_0c9199f14e62.tif; no DrivenData slot spent. Evidence: holdout_round7.json · round7-feature-build.json.

What is actually new — and what is not. The distinctness table was a surprise: 10 m slope and gradient aggregated to 100 m correlate 0.80–0.97 with the supplied det_elev_slope (B19), i.e. the sponsor's slope band was evidently already computed from high-resolution topography. The genuinely new content is the one-sided scarp asymmetry (onesided 0.26, onesided3 0.18, steep_ratio_max 0.41 max |corr|). The distinct-only arm with just those three channels still wins 4/4 on both protocols (dense +0.0139, sparse +0.0076) — so more than half the gain is scarp shape, not slope magnitude. This was recorded in the register as an amendment before the gate ran.

Why H32 should catch faults missing from USGS/INGENIOUS: a 1–10 m fault scarp is a step over 20–100 m — sub-pixel on the 100 m grid but a resolved one-sided gradient ridge at 10 m. The catalogue is stated to be incomplete, most Great Basin quadrangles were mapped before lidar, and the organizers themselves ship 1m_DEM_links.csv, pointing at high-resolution topography as the data class experts use. Null hypotheses: shorelines of pluvial Lake Lahontan, terraces, roads and canal banks are also 1–10 m steps — the holdout gain is measured on catalogue traces, not on the hidden expert truth.

Other Round-7 candidates (registered, not scored): H33 spatial-context U-Net on CPU (reference solution scores 0.1847 publicly; PyTorch CPU wheel unreachable from this sandbox — runner job planned); H34 strike-integrated line contrast over 1.5–2.5 km baselines; H35 GeoDAWN radiometrics K/eTh/eU/TC (ScienceBase, public domain ↗, runner bridge needed); H36 USGS SGMC bedrock faults (not locally validatable). Round-6: Seismic Proximity, Gravity Curvature, Conductivity Fabric, Magnetic Texture

Five new mechanism-distinct candidates H25–H29 preregistered before coding (register). All below 0.50 max |corr| vs rounds 1–5: H25 0.4301 vs shear, H26 0.336 vs |grad iso_grav_hg|, H27 0.339 vs |grad cond|, H28 0.384 vs tmi_hg, H29 0.120 vs intersection density. Frozen masked holdout, NMS-3 @2%, both truth protocols, leakage probe 0.0000 every fold. H28 magnetic texture variance (mag_anom local std 11 px) is the only arm to beat multi25+NMS on ≥3/4 folds on BOTH protocols — dense 3/4 (0.22143 vs 0.22138), sparse 3/4 (0.11438 vs 0.11178, +0.0026). H25 passes sparse 3/4 but dense 1/4; others fail. New artifact 12GEMSDOE_r6-nms3-h28-texture_8721329b55c7.tif packaged; no DrivenData slot spent. Evidence: holdout_round6.json · feature-build.json.

Why H28 should catch faults missing from USGS/INGENIOUS: Hydrothermal alteration destroys magnetite along fault damage zones, creating disrupted magnetic texture (high local variance) rather than clean edge. USGS QFFD requires surface geomorphic scarps; subsurface alteration halo beneath non-magnetic alluvium is invisible to surface mapping but visible to magnetics. H28 uses mag_anom (B1) local standard deviation in 11 px window, distinct from all prior transforms (max |corr| 0.384 vs tmi_hg). Layers: B1 only. Physical signature: texture disruption, not edge.

Other R6 candidates: H25 seismic proximity (B10 deq + B7/B8/B4) — first use of deq_n100a15, targets blind seismically active faults; H26 gravity-slope curvature (B5 + B12) — buried density step; H27 conductivity anisotropy (B17) — fluid-filled conduit fabric; H29 grav-mag decorrelation (B13×B2) — correlation breakdown in damage zones. All viable, none pass both protocols except H28. External H30 1m DEM (USGS 3DEP via AWS S3 s3://usgs-3dep public domain) and H31 Sentinel-2 L2A (s3://sentinel-cogs free/open) verified free/open but transport blocked in-sandbox.

Round-5 geological candidates: honest negative. H20 magnetic-basement step, H21 two-depth-surface step coincidence, H22 local singularity exponent and H23 asymmetric-step detector were preregistered (register) after identity checks showed that tc (B5) is the official top-of-crustal magnetic source depth estimate, not the "tilt angle or total curvature" its raster description claims (strictly positive, median 18.48, correlation −0.0012 with |tilt|). With the emission policy fixed, no channel cleared the ≥3/4-fold gate on both protocols (best: H22 3/4 dense, 2/4 sparse; H15 2/4 sparse). All arms now land within ±0.0008 (dense) and ±0.0018 (sparse) of each other — an order of magnitude below the emission effect. That is the actionable conclusion: stop paying for small feature gains while the decision layer throws away a factor of 2.5.
